OEKO-TEX vs REACH: what EU textile buyers actually need

If you import curtains, cushions or bedding into the EU, two names come up constantly: OEKO-TEX® Standard 100 and EU REACH. They are not the same thing — and asking for the wrong one wastes time. Here's the plain version.

REACH is the law; OEKO-TEX is the proof

REACH is an EU regulation. It restricts certain hazardous substances (SVHCs) in products placed on the European market. It is not optional — if you sell into the EU, your products must comply. But REACH itself doesn't hand you a certificate; it sets the limits.

OEKO-TEX® Standard 100 is a voluntary, third-party testing standard. A lab tests the actual article against a long list of harmful substances — often stricter than REACH's baseline. The result is a certificate with a test report you can show retailers and customers.

What to actually request from a supplier

  • A valid OEKO-TEX® Standard 100 certificate with a current validity date and the correct product class (Class I for baby, II for direct skin contact like cushions/bedding).
  • A REACH compliance statement confirming SVHC limits are met for the EU.
  • A batch test report — not just a generic certificate — so the specific fabric you're buying was tested.

Common mistake: a certificate that doesn't match the fabric

Some suppliers show a certificate for a different fabric or an expired one. Always check the article/material scope and the date. At Yvonne Home we issue test reports per fabric batch and keep them on file for reorders.

The bottom line

You need REACH compliance by law, and OEKO-TEX® (or equivalent) as the evidence buyers trust. Ask for both, matched to the exact product and batch, and your EU entry stays clean.
